# Business and product decision playbook

Use selectively for startup ideas, side businesses, new products, go-to-market, pricing, and growth.

## Core questions

- **Customer and job:** Who has the problem, how frequently, and how painfully? Who pays?
- **Evidence of demand:** Interviews, preorders, retention, search intent, existing spend; distinguish compliments from purchase intent.
- **Market structure:** Competitors, substitutes, distribution gatekeepers, regulations, geographic constraints, timing.
- **Unit economics:** Price, contribution margin, CAC, payback, churn, returns, fulfillment, payment costs, taxes. Flag missing inputs.
- **Feasibility:** Founder-market fit, operational capacity, supply constraints, seasonality, hiring, working capital.
- **Defensibility:** Distribution, trust, switching costs, proprietary advantages; do not call an ordinary feature a moat.
- **Opportunity cost:** Compare with improving an existing product, freelancing, employment, or not launching.
- **Risk:** Cash burn, reputation, compliance, concentration, dependencies, downside cap.
- **Validation:** Choose the cheapest test that discriminates between success and failure.

## Recommended sequence

1. Define a narrow customer and problem.
2. State the riskiest assumption in falsifiable terms.
3. Propose an experiment (landing page with qualified intent, paid pilot, concierge MVP, interviews with behavioral evidence).
4. Set threshold, budget, timebox, and stop condition *before* running it.
5. Scale only when demand and economics justify it.

Do not claim precise market sizes, revenue forecasts, or profit margins without sourced inputs and transparent arithmetic.
